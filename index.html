The Mystery That Was Never Solved

Jack the Ripper was the pseudonym given to an unidentified serial killer responsible for multiple murders committed in the impoverished Whitechapel district of London's East End in 1888. The brutality of the crimes and the failure of the police to apprehend the culprit created a media sensation that continues to captivate public interest worldwide over a century later.

London in 1888

During the Victorian era, Whitechapel was synonymous with extreme poverty, overcrowded lodging houses, unemployment, and crime. Survival for many women meant navigating dangerous streets at night or engaging in informal sex work. The complete absence of modern forensics, fingerprint analysis, or coordinated DNA records left law enforcement severely underequipped to stop a calculated predator.

The Canonical Five Victims

1. Mary Ann Nichols (Polly)

Age: 43 | Died: August 31, 1888
Location: Buck's Row

Recognized as the first canonical victim, Nichols was discovered in the early morning hours. Her murder sparked immediate panic across the district.

2. Annie Chapman

Age: 47 | Died: September 8, 1888
Location: 29 Hanbury Street

Discovered in a residential backyard, Chapman's murder intensified public pressure on the Metropolitan Police and local authorities.

3. Elizabeth Stride

Age: 44 | Died: September 30, 1888
Location: Berner Street

Found in Dutfield's Yard. Historians believe the killer may have been startled or interrupted before finishing the attack.

4. Catherine Eddowes

Age: 46 | Died: September 30, 1888
Location: Mitre Square

Killed on the exact same night as Elizabeth Stride—an event recorded historically as the "Double Event" within the City of London police jurisdiction.

5. Mary Jane Kelly

Age: ~25 | Died: November 9, 1888
Location: Miller's Court

The youngest and final canonical victim. Unlike the others outdoors, Kelly was brutally slain inside her own rented living quarters.

Prominent Historical Suspects

Montague John Druitt

A barrister and schoolteacher whose suicide shortly after the final murder raised police suspicion in internal memoranda.

Aaron Kosminski

A Polish-born Jewish barber living in Whitechapel who was named by senior police officials as a strong suspect.

James Maybrick

A cotton merchant whose controversial 1990s diary claimed direct responsibility for the murders, though authenticity remains heavily debated.

Francis Tumblety

An eccentric American-born self-proclaimed doctor who fled Britain amid intense police scrutiny.

Frequently Asked Questions

Was Jack the Ripper ever identified?

No. Despite exhaustive police investigations and hundreds of suspects proposed over the years, the killer's true identity remains entirely unknown.

Why is he called "Jack the Ripper"?

The name originated from a sensational letter sent to the Central News Agency in September 1888, signed by someone claiming to be the murderer.

How many victims are officially recognized?

Five women (Nichols, Chapman, Stride, Eddowes, and Kelly) are traditionally recognized as the "Canonical Five" victims.
